This file records the original requested changes. See README.md for implementation details.
Follow-up direction: implement local university-issued login and in-app notifications;
ERP/SSO and outbound email/external department delivery are explicitly deferred.
The revised direction supersedes public student signup as the main entry flow.

1. SIMPLIFY ENTRY: UNIVERSITY-PROVISIONED ACCOUNTS
The university ERP and IT team handle admission and account provisioning.
Students should arrive with a university-issued identity and simply sign in,
or enter through university single sign-on once that integration is agreed.
Remove public student account creation and repeated onboarding from the normal
student experience. Do not make students re-enter details the ERP already owns.
Keep a clearly labelled local demonstration login until real integration exists.
Existing users should return directly to their current research position.
Continuing students must retain verified prior progress; no forced restart.
Provisioning must establish programme, cohort, study mode, roles and scope.
Do not silently delete existing records when retiring the old signup flow.

2. SUPERVISION FIRST, THEN THE JOURNEY
For a student without a confirmed supervisor, prominently show requesting
supervision as the next action. The Supervision tab should show eligible people,
request status, acceptance/confirmation and the final assigned supervisor(s).
Make it obvious who the student received and what they should do next.
Preserve capacity, eligibility and authorization checks and assignment history.
Dean, HOD and other authorized academic staff handle confirmations and decisions
within their scopes, including supervision, leave/extensions and interventions.
Confirm the university's primary/secondary supervision rules before changing
the existing two-position allocation policy.

3. REPLACE THE LOGO
Use the supplied horizontal Primary Logo.png in the appropriate branding areas.
Preserve aspect ratio and readable university lettering on desktop and mobile.
Check sign-in, header and mobile navigation; avoid squeezing the full wordmark
into a tiny square. Make an explicit suitable favicon choice.

4. STAFF DASHBOARDS: SUBTLE, MEANINGFUL SIGNALS
Retain the neat layout, but add restrained visual signals to metrics such as
overdue reviews and students awaiting allocation. Lower overdue counts are
better; elevated counts should visibly indicate attention is needed.
Use text as well as colour, with agreed thresholds rather than arbitrary alarm.
Improve progress-by-department summaries so the progress is easier to interpret.
Do not add decoration where it does not convey useful information.

5. GROUP STAFF MILESTONES BY STUDENT
Replace scattered milestone cards with student cards sorted alphabetically.
Show identity, current research context and a compact progress-percentage circle.
Allow each card to expand into that student's milestones, statuses and relevant
concept/topic information. Each milestone must still open its full record.
Keep all results restricted to the viewer's assigned academic scope.

6. REALISTIC, CONNECTED DEMONSTRATION CONTENT
Provide explicitly fictional but realistic IT-related research tasks for the
demo student, including preparing and submitting a first research concept.
Include useful resource guidance, potentially referring to Agenda 2063 where
academically relevant. Verify resource links and do not present example guidance
as official university policy.
Show a believable mixture of completed, current and future work. Completed
tasks should have readable completion states and green checks.
Populate relevant resources, comments, submissions, review feedback and
notifications so a presenter can demonstrate the experience without empty pages.
Use genuine linked demo records, not hard-coded visual completion labels.
Make student actions and their resulting staff records visible across accounts.
Preserve existing data; additions should be safe to repeat without duplicates.

7. RESEARCH CONCEPT HISTORY
Show the different concepts or concept versions submitted by the student.
Clearly distinguish approved, declined and changes-requested outcomes; use an
X for declined and a positive marker for approved, with text labels as well.
Do not label a request for revisions as a final rejection.
Show reviewer, decision date/time, feedback and the exact version reviewed.
Prefill illustrative concept outcomes in demo data and show the same history
in authorized dean/staff views as neat, readable cards.

8. SUBMISSION COMMENTS AND CHAT-LIKE DISCUSSIONS
Place a conversation thread directly after submissions within the milestone.
Students and authorized staff should be able to read and reply, with author,
timestamp and submission/version context retained.
Make new replies discoverable through notifications and appropriate email alerts.
Support referral/escalation to an authorized colleague with recorded ownership
and history; clarify whether 'transfer' means reassignment, mention or referral.
Do not grant all senior staff unrestricted access to every conversation.
Keep restricted welfare discussions separate from ordinary academic comments.
Define and test how updates appear (refresh/polling or real-time delivery),
including unread state, failed sends, retry behaviour and duplicate prevention.

9. ACTIONABLE NEXT-ACTION OWNER
Show the person or department actually responsible for clearing the delay.
If a review is late, identify the responsible reviewer; if a submission is
pending, identify the student. Administrative issues can name IT, finance or
another configured department as appropriate.
Make the owner/contact control open a small message composer with recipient
and context preselected. The staff member reviews and sends the message.
Internal recipients should receive an in-app notification and configured email;
external departmental contacts may receive email without needing a portal account.
Show delivery state accurately: queued, sent to provider or failed, rather than
claiming delivery simply because the message was saved.
Record sender, recipient, context and follow-up history. Use authorized contact
data and prevent arbitrary bulk mail or exposure of private case information.

10. NOTIFICATIONS AND EMAIL
Verify end-to-end in-app notifications for comments, assigned actions, due work,
overdue reviews and relevant academic/support decisions.
Add actual outbound email through an approved provider or SMTP configuration.
Support periodic reminders/digests for students and relevant dean/staff users,
with agreed recipients, frequency and escalation rules.
Use durable delivery jobs, retry handling and duplicate suppression. Avoid
repeated alerts for already completed work or approved leave.
Keep sensitive narrative content out of email; prefer safe summaries and links.
Test with a local mail capture service or approved test inbox before live use.
Fictional demo addresses must never be used to demonstrate real email delivery.
Provider acceptance is not proof of inbox delivery; report only verified state.

11. LEAVE AND EXTENSIONS
Investigate and fix the leave/extension experience end to end: student request,
authorized staff review, approval/decline, feedback and reflected schedule changes.
Ensure requests open correctly and permitted actions are clear at every state.
Pending requests must not alter deadlines. Approved changes must preserve
original dates, reasons and decision history. Confirm return-from-leave handling.
Check deadline flags, reports and reminders against the resulting schedule.
The feedback did not identify a specific failing case; reproduce and test before
claiming a particular root cause.

12. ERP AND OTHER INTEGRATIONS — INFORMATION REQUIRED
ERP product/vendor, test environment and university technical contact.
Authentication method (for example OIDC/SAML) versus data synchronization method
(API, webhook or controlled import); these are separate integration concerns.
Approved account/student identifiers, field mappings, role/scope ownership,
account disablement, continuing-student progress and synchronization policies.
API documentation, authorized credentials, callback details and sample test data.
Approved email provider, sender identity, domain configuration and test recipients.
Actual contact addresses for departments such as IT and finance.
An explicit list of 'other integrations' and desired outcomes before expanding
scope to calendars, Teams, Turnitin, SMS or similar services.
Never simulate a successful ERP or external-service connection in the UI.
Request credentials through secure configuration, not this requirements file.

13. PROPOSED IMPLEMENTATION ORDER
First: branding, simplified login presentation, supervision-first guidance,
student-grouped staff milestones and restrained dashboard status indicators.
Second: concept history, linked demo content, comments and request-flow fixes.
Third: accountable follow-up messaging and verified in-app notification behaviour.
Fourth: configured email delivery and university-approved ERP integration,
followed by any separately agreed integrations.
Keep local demonstrability distinct from production integration readiness.

14. ACCEPTANCE WALKTHROUGH
A provisioned new student signs in without public registration, requests a
supervisor, sees the request accepted and confirmed, then proceeds with research.
A returning student resumes the correct stage with prior work intact.
Student concepts and submissions appear to the right supervisor and academic
managers with accurate version, reviewer and decision history.
A comment appears in the correct thread and notifies the permitted recipient.
A staff member follows up with the next action owner through the composer;
the recipient receives the in-app message and, when configured, a test email.
Staff can expand an alphabetically grouped student card and open a milestone.
An approved leave/extension updates the schedule, flags and reports consistently.
Restricted records remain inaccessible to unauthorized accounts.
Desktop and mobile flows work, including empty/loading/error and failed-send cases.
Run automated regression checks with a disposable database, preserving demo data.
