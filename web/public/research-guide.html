From research idea to proposal

Fictional demonstration guidance, not official university policy. Confirm requirements with your supervisor and programme office.

Prepare your concept

  1. Describe a specific information-systems problem: for example, barriers faced by small businesses using county digital services.
  2. Identify the affected population, what is already known and the gap your study addresses.
  3. Write a focused aim and measurable objectives. Ensure each objective can be answered with the proposed evidence.
  4. Outline your methodology, feasible sample, consent process and data-protection approach.
  5. Submit through Research concepts and respond to your supervisor's recorded feedback.

Develop the proposal

Create a literature matrix with columns for source, question, method, findings and remaining gap. Explain why your proposed method answers your research question. Avoid claiming findings before collecting evidence.

Development context

You may explore digital inclusion and public-service access in the context of Africa's Agenda 2063. Consult the official framework through your library or supervisor, cite the source you actually read, and explain its relevance rather than assuming alignment.

Before submission

Check citations, objectives, sampling, ethics, consent and the schedule. Upload a new version when revising. Attach similarity evidence where required. Discuss academic questions in the milestone thread; use private support for personal matters.
